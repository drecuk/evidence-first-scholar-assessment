# Peer benchmarking and decision

## Peer selection

Choose peers before calculating comparative results. For detailed qualitative context, select four to eight scholars matched as closely as feasible on domain/subfield, problem and methods, career stage or years since first publication, expected appointment level, research system where relevant, and output culture.

Do not choose only famous outliers or convenient local colleagues. Administrative titles are not a matching criterion.

Apply equivalent databases, work types, date windows, field filters, identity cleaning, and missing-value rules. Flag classification problems and coverage asymmetries. If the candidate corpus is cleaned more strictly than peers, disclose the imbalance and avoid false precision.

Use median, range, and interquartile range. Report matched-cohort rank and ratio/difference to median, not a precise percentile. A percentile requires a separate sufficiently large, transparently defined reference population with uncertainty.

Run at least these sensitivities:

- confirmed-only versus confirmed-plus-probable candidate works;
- narrow versus broad field definition;
- full versus fractional counting in team-heavy fields;
- recent versus career windows;
- self-citation adjustment where reliable;
- alternative provider or classification where feasible.

## Default research-only scorecard

Use a score only when the decision context requires one and freeze the weights before seeing results.

| Dimension | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Research quality, originality, and rigour | 30% |
| Scholarly influence and durability | 25% |
| Research programme, trajectory, and currency | 15% |
| Candidate contribution and intellectual leadership | 15% |
| Scholarly reach, reuse, and collaboration quality | 10% |
| Robustness, openness, and research integrity | 5% |

For a 0–5 expert rating:

```text
weighted points = (rating / 5) * dimension weight
total score = sum(weighted points)
```

Do not map metrics mechanically into points. Metrics inform the rating, reveal anomalies, and test the narrative. Provide lower/central/upper scenarios based on identified uncertainties such as corpus ambiguity, provider coverage, expert disagreement, field classification, or unresolved contribution evidence. This is not a statistical confidence interval unless derived as one.

Rating anchors:

| Rating | Meaning |
|---:|---|
| 0 | No credible evidence or material contradiction |
| 1 | Very limited evidence; substantially below appropriate peers |
| 2 | Some credible evidence, but weak, dated, narrow, or poorly attributable |
| 3 | Solid established performance, broadly credible and field-appropriate |
| 4 | Strong, sustained, peer-competitive evidence with clear contributions |
| 5 | Exceptional field-leading evidence with robust attribution and corroboration |

Thresholds are local, not universal. Lead with the evidence rather than the number.

## Senior or chair-level gates

For a research-intensive senior or chair appointment, assess four non-compensatory gates separately:

1. **Landmark research:** at least one contribution is independently judged world-leading, agenda-setting, or a primary reference in the appropriate field.
2. **Sustained distinction:** strong research spans career and recent windows; one early landmark is insufficient by itself.
3. **Attributable intellectual leadership:** decisive conceptual, methodological, analytical, empirical, software, validation, or original-writing contribution is verified.
4. **Research reliability:** no unresolved identity, attribution, integrity, correction, or evidentiary concern materially undermines the record.

A citation total, h-index, publication count, grant total, famous collaborator, or prestigious venue cannot compensate for a failed gate.

## Decision standard

A defensible assessment lets a skeptical reader answer:

- Who is the candidate bibliographically?
- What did the candidate contribute?
- How was output quality assessed independently?
- Which work influenced the field, how broadly, and how recently?
- How does the record compare with genuinely similar peers under common rules?
- Is intellectual leadership demonstrated rather than inferred from seniority or author position?
- Is the programme coherent, active, robust, appropriately open/auditable, and responsibly corrected?
- What is verified, indicative, claimed, contradicted, or not established?
- What new evidence could change the result?
- Can the numbers and material claims be reproduced from the case files?

If these questions cannot be answered, record an evidence gap rather than inventing a conclusion.
