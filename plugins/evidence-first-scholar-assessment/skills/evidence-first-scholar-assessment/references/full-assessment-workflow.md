# Full assessment workflow

## Phase 0 — Freeze the protocol

Create the case workspace before extensive searching. Preserve supplied inputs, start the source manifest and search log, and record:

- assessment and citation snapshot dates;
- candidate, domain, subfield, career stage, and decision context;
- inclusion/exclusion rules and output types;
- five- and ten-complete-year windows, with the current partial year separate;
- peer-selection and expert-reviewer rules;
- metric definitions and any score weights;
- database access and coverage limitations;
- planned outputs and completion gates.

Do not change rules after viewing results unless the change is documented, justified, and applied symmetrically.

## Phase 1 — Resolve identity

Build an identity record containing name variants, initials, native-script names, affiliations, topics, frequent coauthors, identifiers, and career time range. Assign every work one status:

- **Confirmed:** title/identifier and multiple identity signals fit.
- **Probable:** multiple signals fit without contradiction.
- **Ambiguous:** common-name collision, missing metadata, or weak fit.
- **Excluded:** namesake, duplicate, correction, non-scholarly item, or out-of-scope output.

Core metrics use confirmed works. Put probable works in a declared sensitivity analysis. Preserve exclusions and ambiguity decisions.

Warning signs include abrupt topic changes, impossible affiliations, disconnected coauthor networks, profile splits or merges, duplicated translations or versions, abstract/final-paper duplication, and database undercoverage of books or non-English work.

## Phase 2 — Build the verified bibliography

Create a work-level dataset with stable identifiers, normalized titles and dates, output type, authorship, contribution evidence, source-specific citations, normalized indicators, field/subfield, collaboration, funding, reuse, and notes. Keep raw records separate from analyst-curated rows.

Do not assume every CV item is indexed or every indexed item belongs to the candidate. Trace each included row to a raw response, primary record, or candidate-supplied document.

## Phase 3 — Analyze the research programme

Read, where accessible:

- three to five candidate-identified signature outputs with contribution statements;
- the five most cited works;
- the five most recent substantive works;
- claimed breakthroughs or programme-defining works;
- outputs central to a theory, method, dataset, software, benchmark, or sustained line of research.

Cluster the record into three to six coherent strands. For each, describe the question, conceptual contribution, method/evidence, important finding, development over time, scholarly uptake, candidate role, and limitations or contested claims. Construct a research chronology based on intellectual development rather than administrative appointments.

## Phase 3A — Independent output assessment

For a consequential appointment-grade decision, seek at least two independent, conflict-checked expert assessments for each signature output, with appropriate subfield and international coverage. Ask each reviewer to rate 0–5 and justify:

- originality;
- scholarly significance;
- rigour;
- attributable candidate contribution;
- durability.

Preserve reviewer ranges and substantive disagreement. Do not infer quality from venue, citations, institutional affiliation, awards, or reviewer prestige.

An AI may provide a clearly labeled provisional critical reading, identify issues for expert attention, and synthesize genuine reviews. It must not impersonate experts, invent reviewers, or manufacture consensus. If genuine reviews are unavailable, reduce confidence and mark the evidence gap.

## Phase 4 — Calculate indicators

Use the validated corpus and the rules in [evidence-and-metrics.md](evidence-and-metrics.md). Report numerators, denominators, provider, snapshot date, corpus rules, and missingness. Separate current activity from cumulative legacy influence. Interpret every figure in discipline and career context.

## Phase 5 — Cross-database validation

For five to ten consequential outputs, match by DOI and normalized title, capture source-specific citation counts on approximately the same date, explain discrepancies, and verify authorship, corresponding-author status, funding acknowledgement, and document type on a primary record. Convergence supports confidence in a citation core; it does not make either provider exact.

## Phase 6 — Select and validate peers

Freeze the peer rule before results. Use four to eight close comparators for detailed context and a separate sufficiently large reference population if percentiles are required. Follow [peer-benchmarking-and-decision.md](peer-benchmarking-and-decision.md).

## Phase 7 — Verify funding context

Use one row per award. Verify funder, scheme, identifier, dates, amount/currency, candidate role, co-investigators, linked outputs, and evidence status. Credit only the formal role in the award record. Do not infer PI status or allocate the full award to every investigator. Funding is an input, not a direct research-quality score.

## Optional module — External impact

Include industry, policy, clinical, technical, professional, or public impact only when separately requested. Test the chain:

```text
research output -> candidate contribution -> external user -> adoption/use
-> observable change -> measured outcome -> independent confirmation
```

Keep findings and any score outside the core research score. Avoid assigning team/project impact wholly to one scholar.

## Phase 8 — Synthesize and decide

Use qualitative judgment supported by evidence and metrics. If scoring is required, freeze the rubric before evaluation and provide lower/central/upper sensitivity scenarios based on identified uncertainties. Report research-only gates separately from the score.

## Phase 9 — Adversarial and reproducibility audit

Test inclusion/exclusion of probable works, broad/narrow fields, self-citation adjustments where available, career-length and fractional-count effects, provider convergence, normalized-metric stability, citation concentration, older-work dominance, peer symmetry, negative searches, and claim-to-source traceability. Rerun calculations from archived inputs.

## Phase 10 — Assemble and hand off

Freeze curated tables, generate metrics and sensitivity outputs, complete claims and evidence-gap ledgers, write the full report, derive the brief, reconcile values, render and inspect the requested document, and finish README/run-log/QA records. Mark the case complete, complete with limitations, or provisional.
